# Trading Securities

## Market Makers and Bid-Ask Spread

Market makers earn the bid-ask spread as compensation for inventory risks.

- **More liquid** securities have **less riskier** positions for market makers → **smaller bid-ask spread**
- Liquidity $\iff$ Spread (inverse relationship)

### Low Cost Trading

Market makers face different levels of risk depending on the traders:

- **Uninformed retail traders** - Low information advantage
- **Informed trading funds** - High information advantage

High-volume orders from informed hedge funds imply significant price changes. Market makers:
- Charge **less** from retail traders (lower risk)
- Skip orders from hedge funds (to reduce risks)

### Order Types

#### Market Order
- Buy or Sell at prevailing prices
- Immediate execution at current market rates

#### Limit Order
- Buy or Sell only if specific price conditions are met
- Limitation: Price may drop for no reason and then rise again unexpectedly
- Solution: Map between news and price movements to identify real bad news versus market mistakes

## Buying on Margin

Leverage: Borrowing funds from broker to buy securities

### Key Metrics

**Margin:** The percentage funded by the investor

$$\text{Margin}=\frac{\text{Account Equity}}{\text{Account Market Value}}$$

- **Equity:** What you can walk away with (current account value minus debt)
- **Margin Call:** When maintenance margin is not met, you must fund the account or the broker sells margined securities
- **New Position Calculation:** Required after margin call

### Effects

- **Magnifies Returns:** Either way (positive or negative)
- **Maintenance Margin:** Brokers set this based on:
  - Correlation within the portfolio
  - Risk profile of the securities

### Risks

Buying on margin involves significant risks due to leveraging effects on both gains and losses.

## Short Selling

Borrow securities from broker and then sell them

### Mechanics

- **Strategy:** Sell now (at high price), buy later (at low price)
- **Liability:** Loans are in terms of shares (you owe the stock itself, not cash)
- **Margin Required:** Must be funded at the beginning
- **Implicit Leverage:** Short selling acts as a form of leverage

### Key Differences from Buying on Margin

- Short selling borrows securities, not cash
- Liability is denominated in shares, not dollars
